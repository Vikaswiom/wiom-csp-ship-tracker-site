The release adoption framework

Every CSP app release becomes a task that users must understand and finish on their own. Each one is measured the same way. Leading metrics give an early warning in the first days after launch, and lagging metrics show by Day 30 whether the task really worked.

Metric tree

Read left to right. Users must be reached, discover the task, act on it and get help if stuck (leading). That drives completion, adoption depth, outcomes and quality (lagging). Each release names its objective and picks its own north star from the catalog (for STV: false closes that reached the customer). Completed unaided, at least 90% by Day 30, is the adoption goal every release must also meet.

Design lens: six questions every release must answer

Business metrics tell us whether a release worked. The design lens tells us why: whether CSPs could understand and use it on their own. Each catalog metric that answers one of these questions is tagged with it, and every release's Design scorecard shows them together. A question with no metric shows as “Not measured”, which is a gap in the release's tracking to fix before launch.

1 · Understand the change
Saw the launch explainer; understood it (quiz right first time).
2 · Know what happened
Noticed the task within 1 hour and within 48 hours.
3 · Know what to do
Finished their part; did it themselves in the app; finished in one sitting; passed first time; failure rate.
4 · Know what Wiom will do next
Came back to check while waiting; closed as time over.
5 · Recover and get help
Recovered after a failed check; needed help; helpline calls per 1,000; completed after help.
6 · Learn to finish alone
Completed unaided (north star); unaided on first 3 tasks vs once experienced; CSPs at 90% or more unaided.

Design asks for every release: an in-app explainer with a one-question check, an app event for each screen and tap of the task (opened with entry source, action taken, status checked), and a status message for every wait.

Release lifecycle

1 · PlanBefore launchWrite the release brief: the objective, its north star metric, the Linear ticket and PRDs, who it is for, what successful use means, which catalog metrics apply, Day 0/7/30 targets and the baseline group. Name the owner.
2 · ReadyLaunch gateThe owner confirms training is done, the helpline has a script and a Kapture category, and the funnel query is in the daily refresh.
3 · Launch weekDay 0–6Watch leading metrics daily: reach, seen, user finished their part, failure rate, helpline calls per 1,000.
4 · Day 7 reviewDay 7–29Compare leading metrics with Day 7 targets. The biggest drop-off gets a named fixer and a plan on the weekly card.
5 · Day 30 reviewDay 30Judge lagging metrics. Adopted if the north star meets its Day 30 target and completed unaided is at least 90%; otherwise the release stays on the card as Needs fix.

Launch checklist for the named owner

  • Release brief written: the objective, its north star metric, who it is for and what successful use means, in the user's words.
  • On every screen of the task, users can tell what happened, what they need to do and what Wiom will do next.
  • Targets set for Day 0, Day 7 and Day 30, with the baseline group named.
  • Helpline trained, with a script and a Kapture category for the release.
  • Support channel ready for users who still need help, and completion after help tracked.
  • Funnel query written and added to the daily refresh before Day 0.
  • Every rollout step and config change logged with its date, so metric shifts can be read against it.

Bringing a new release onto the tracker

  1. Add the release in Manage: name, app, Day 0 date, who it is for, successful use, owner, training and support.
  2. Map its task onto the catalog stages: what counts as reached, seen, user finished their part, failed and completed. Add a catalog metric if the release needs one that doesn't exist yet.
  3. Write its funnel query so it returns one row per cohort, day and metric, with a numerator and a denominator, using the catalog's metric ids. The baseline is the same task on the old flow, or the same users before launch.
  4. Add the query to the daily refresh. Its rows appear here under the release's id with no change to the page.
  5. Set any targets that differ from the catalog defaults.

Data notes
  • STV · Service ticket verification. Users = service tickets on the STV flow (TAS stv_mode). Baseline = old-flow tickets received on the same days; STV CSPs come from a rollout list, so they are not a random sample. STV holds every ticket for 24 hours of verification before it closes, so “closed within 4 working hours” and “closed within 24 hours” are near zero by design. Use “user finished within 4 working hours” to compare speed.
  • Helpline. CSP helpline calls whose Kapture ticket is filed under TICKETS or CSP APP. 89% of calls match a CSP.
  • Repeat complaint. A new complaint (a different complaint id) on the same connection within 7 days of close.
  • Source. Snowflake through Metabase, saved as a snapshot. Open Sources to see each query and download rows.
